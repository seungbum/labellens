LabelLens

How the census and the lenses work, what they cannot see, and how the data is handled.

InspectNetworkAbout

Stackable moderation in one paragraph

On Bluesky, moderation is split into labels. Any account can run a labeling service that attaches short values such as spam or ai-imagery to posts and accounts. Each user subscribes to up to twenty such services on top of Bluesky's own, and chooses per label whether to hide, warn or ignore. Two people looking at the same post can therefore see different things, and nobody, including the author, sees the whole picture.

The census

When you inspect an item, your browser sends one com.atproto.label.queryLabels request to every labeler in the directory, asking about the post, its author's account and the author's profile record. Answers are folded into the labels in force now: a later negation retracts a label, and a label past its expiry time no longer applies. Labeler services that are offline are reported as unreachable, and so are those that do not allow browser requests (no CORS header). For the two example accounts only, the labels those services had issued at the last measurement are shown instead, marked snapshot; the site does not republish such labels for anyone else.

The lenses

Each lens is a set of subscriptions. LabelLens feeds the census result and the labelers' own label definitions into the moderation code of the official Bluesky client library (@atproto/api) and reports what that code decides for a feed (posts) or a profile list (accounts), with default preferences and adult content off. The lenses are:

  • Logged-out visitor: Bluesky's own moderation only. This is also what the public read-only API returns: it ignores requests for third-party labels.
  • Popular stack: the twenty most-liked labelers.
  • Protective stack: the twenty most-liked labelers that declare at least one label which hides, blurs or warns.
  • Your stack: any combination you pick from the labelers that labelled the item.

Limits

  • The directory is a snapshot (see the footer date) of labeler services declared on the network.
  • A labeler's answer is what its server returns now. LabelLens does not verify label signatures.
  • Lenses use default preferences; real users may have changed per-label settings, muted words or blocks.
  • Labels with values a labeler never declared are shown but have no effect, because clients ignore them.

Data and ethics

LabelLens only reads public data through public APIs, needs no login and stores nothing: every request goes from your browser straight to Bluesky or the labeler, so each labeler you query learns your IP address and the item you asked about, as it would from any Bluesky client. The measurement on the Network page reports aggregate figures per labeler service and does not publish lists of labelled accounts or posts. Labels can be wrong or hostile; showing a label here does not endorse it.
